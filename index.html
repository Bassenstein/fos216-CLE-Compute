Executive summary

Use the pause to write a program, not just a rule.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

Cleveland paused new standalone data centers on July 15 to figure out how to regulate them. That was the right call. This deck proposes what should come out the other side: CLE COMPUTE, a City-run industry development program that decides where compute can go, makes sure residents never pay for its power, pulls the factories that supply it into our industrial corridors, and builds an AI education ladder from kindergarten to post-graduate research.

00 · Power

Bring your own power

Large campuses build or buy their own generation. Cleveland Public Power customers are shielded from the cost.

01 · Host

Data-Manufacturing campuses

A 200 MW data center plus 1–2 supply-chain factories on each campus, in existing and new industrial zones, under strict noise and discharge standards.

02 · Make

Supply-chain factories

Recruit makers of cooling, power, electrical, and fiber equipment. This is where most of the durable jobs are.

03 · Teach

K–PhD AI pipeline

AI literacy in every grade, trades and AI pathways at CMSD, credentials at Tri-C, degrees and research at our universities.

Starting point: the Administration’s guardrails

We agree with the Mayor. Build the program on his terms.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001
Hyperscale data centers “have no future” in dense neighborhoods.— Mayor Justin M. Bibb, as reported by Ideastream, May 27, 2026

Cleveland already has industrial zones inside its neighborhoods. CLE COMPUTE would create more of them, and anything built there must meet strict noise and environmental-discharge mitigation. That rules out the conventional campus: a flat box with open cooling yards, dropped in without residents at the table.

Noise

Written dBA limits, monitored in public.

Neighborhood impact

Relocation, housing, and amenities first.

Environmental risk

Strict discharge limits; systems enclosed.

Transparency

Public records, live data dashboard.

Zoning conflicts

Creative zoning, defined facility classes.

Community benefit

Jobs, pay, and training guarantees.

The FOS216 Data-Manufacturing Campus™ model: what it delivers

Proprietary · outcomes shown, methods withheld

Housing first. Creative zoning lets new homes rise on vacant lots and new apartment communities open within a 2-mile radius, before anyone moves.

No one pushed out. Every resident in a proposed campus zone is offered new housing inside that same 2-mile radius, with new grocery and retail nearby.

More than a data center. Each campus pairs a 200 MW data center with 1–2 data center supply-chain manufacturers. The factories create the high-paying jobs.

Jobs for neighbors. Relocated residents get first right of refusal on campus jobs, starting at least 10% above the neighborhood’s average household income (never below the citywide $55,045 floor), so they earn more and live in a new home. Training and education lead to up to 3×.

Hyperscale in the urban core. A vertical design with all cooling and mechanical systems inside or on top of the building. No ground-level yards.

Power for the block, not just the building. New generation and transmission on the same campus, serving the campus and the surrounding neighborhood. 65–70% impact mitigation designed in.

Straight talk on jobs

A data center alone is not a jobs program. The industry is.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

New Brookings research found that a county’s first large data center raises data-processing employment by about 100–200 jobs over a decade, and wages did not change. Tax breaks were about 2% of hyperscale construction cost.

So if Cleveland treats compute as “approve a building,” residents get a large power customer and a small staff. If Cleveland treats compute as an industry, the building becomes the anchor customer for factories, trades, and schools.

Program rule: every data center campus includes 1–2 supply-chain manufacturers on the same site. The factories carry the high-paying jobs; the 200 MW data center is their anchor customer.

Pillar 00 · Power, the foundation

One hyperscale campus could equal half of CPP.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

CPP: ~74,000 customers, 36 substations, four 138 kV interconnections, three combustion turbines and ten diesel units (City of Cleveland).

The rule: residents never pay for compute’s power

  • Bring your own power. Large campuses build, bring, or buy the new generation they need. The Ohio Senate’s version of HB 646 would require this at 250 MW and up.
  • Pay for the wires. Developers fund every substation and line upgrade their load requires.
  • Separate rate class so costs land on the data center, not on households.
  • Give back. Campuses that generate on site sell surplus to CPP at peak, and help carry CPP’s substation upgrade program.

These terms match the five commitments in the federal Ratepayer Protection Pledge (March 2026) signed by major tech companies. Cleveland should write them into local law so they do not depend on a pledge.

Why makers would come now

  • Tariffs. A 25% Section 232 tariff on certain advanced chips took effect Jan 14, 2026. A broader Phase 2, possibly covering data center servers, is under review.
  • Duty-free for building here. The Commerce Department is weighing a formula that ties duty-free import allowances to U.S. manufacturing investment.
  • Deadline. The 35% federal advanced manufacturing credit (48D) needs construction started by Dec 31, 2026.

Straight talk: making a product in the U.S. avoids duties on the finished import, but imported components can still carry tariffs. Pitch lower landed cost and faster delivery, not “zero tariffs.”

Pillar 02 · How the City recruits

A supply-chain attraction toolkit Cleveland controls.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

1 · Ready sites

Assemble land bank and brownfield parcels next to each compute territory into pad-ready industrial sites with power, water, and fiber stubbed in.

2 · Anchor-customer rule

Hyperscale tenants in City territories commit a share of purchasing to Cleveland-based suppliers, reported on the scorecard.

3 · Foreign investment desk

One door for overseas makers exposed to tariffs: sites, permits, JobsOhio incentives, visas, and workforce in one package.

4 · Tariff & credit navigator

Help firms use the 48D credit, Section 232 relief paths, and state programs before deadlines pass.

5 · Freight advantage

Market the Port of Cleveland, Class I rail, and I-71/77/90/480 access for heavy power and cooling equipment.

6 · Hire-here pipeline

Every recruited plant gets a trained applicant pool from Pillar 03, with first look for residents near the site.

Suggested first targets: expansions by firms already in the region (power, thermal, and fabrication), then overseas makers of transformers, switchgear, and liquid-cooling hardware that need U.S. production to serve Midwest data center demand.

Pillar 03 · What exists, and what to add

The policy base is already in place. Now fund the ladder.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

Already done

  • Ohio required every K–12 district to adopt an AI policy by July 1, 2026, with a state model policy.
  • CMSD approved its AI policy June 23, 2026 (unanimous), centered on AI literacy, with a working group that includes higher education and business.
  • Case Western Reserve offers a B.S. and B.A. in Artificial Intelligence, AI minors, and 400+ courses integrating AI.
  • Tri-C runs applied AI training and the Center for the Future of Work; Ohio’s TechCred pays for short tech credentials.
  • Federal: the Presidential AI Challenge invites K–12 students and teachers to build AI projects.

Suggested additions

  • Cleveland Compute Education Compact signed by the City, CMSD, Tri-C, CSU, CWRU, Kent State, and Akron with shared targets.
  • Teacher readiness: paid summer AI institutes and a stipend for every CMSD teacher who completes one.
  • Labs in neighborhoods: AI and robotics labs at rec centers and Cleveland Public Library branches, open evenings to families.
  • Student choice: learners work across all the major AI models, not locked to one vendor.
  • Data center training floor inside each territory, so students learn on real power and cooling equipment.
  • Industry-funded seats: each territory tenant sponsors scholarships and apprenticeships.

Community benefit

Neighborhoods first: written down, enforceable, public.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001

Jobs

  • First right of refusal on campus jobs for relocated and nearby residents
  • Starting pay at least 10% above the neighborhood average, with a $55,045 citywide floor
  • Local-hire targets, reported quarterly
  • Workforce shuttle within a 2-mile radius

Housing & place

  • New workforce housing on vacant lots around each territory
  • Relocation within 2 miles for anyone displaced
  • Grocery and retail investment in the area

Protection

  • Surety bond to protect the City if a project fails
  • No NDAs that override public records (Cuyahoga County guidance)
  • Noise and water monitors with open data

City ownership

Territory land stays owned by Cleveland taxpayers and is leased to developers. In return, developers get first right to buy nearby vacant lots for the workforce housing they build, so they earn from it.

Neighborhood fund

A per-megawatt annual payment into a fund governed with residents, for home repair, parks, and youth programs in the surrounding wards.

The rule

Campus jobs start at least 10% above the host neighborhood’s average household income, and never below a citywide floor set at Cleveland’s average, $55,045. One job should out-earn what the whole household earned before.

The ladder

Training and education carry workers to 3× that start: roughly $115,000–$150,000 in most of these neighborhoods, in senior operator, controls, electrical, and technician roles.

Said plainly

These are averages, not medians. Averages run higher, so the floor is higher too. Central’s average is the lowest in the city, which is why the citywide floor exists.

Trust is the program

What CLE COMPUTE will not promise.

The FixOurStreets216 ProjectCLE COMPUTE · FOS216-CLEC-001
Claim you may hearWhat we say instead
“Zero noise.”Noise below a written limit at the property line, measured continuously and posted publicly.
“Thousands of jobs at the data center.”A data hall employs a modest permanent staff. The larger job count comes from construction, grid work, factories, and schools, and we will report actual hires.
“Zero tariffs.”Building here avoids duties on imported finished goods and cuts delivery time. Imported components may still be tariffed.
“No impact on electric bills.”No cost shifted to CPP customers, backed by law, a separate rate class, and a public scorecard, not by a promise.
“Guaranteed fast permits.”A clear, predictable process with published standards. Speed comes from meeting the standard, not from skipping review.
“No one will be displaced.”Any take area is identified in public early, and every household or business is relocated within 2 miles into new housing or space.
